Network notice · Knots 29.4.2 soft fork

From block 973440 (~22 Sep) to 979920 (~5 Nov) every newly mined coinbase must wait 45 days before it can be spent, instead of 100 blocks. It is temporary, and it exists to make hit-and-run mining unprofitable and give miners who stay an edge. Payouts, shares and the split do not change; coins from those blocks just unlock at block 979920 (make-goods too). Lazarus supports it and our nodes switch at block 973400. DATUM gateway operators run their own node, so the choice is yours: to support it, run Knots 29.4.2 before block 973440.

          Learn
          
        
          New to this? Start here.
          
        
          Six short answers, then the long versions if you want them. No account, no sign-up: mining here only ever needs a payout address.
          
      
          
      
          
        
          
          1
          
          Proof of work, in one minute
          
          
          Miners race to find a number that makes a block’s hash fall below a target. It is pure trial and error, so the only way to win more often is to do more hashes. That cost is what makes rewriting the chain expensive. Difficulty retargets so blocks keep arriving at a steady pace however much hashrate joins.
          
          How the pool fits in →
        
          
        
          
          2
          
          Why this chain uses BLAKE2b
          
          
          At block 961,640 (30 August 2026) this chain changed its proof of work from SHA-256d to BLAKE2b. Supply, halvings, addresses and every earlier block are unchanged. BLAKE2b is what Siacoin ASICs were built for, so that hardware mines here.
          
          The fork, and BIP-110 →
        
          
        
          
          3
          
          What DATUM changes
          
          
          On an ordinary pool, the pool decides what goes in the block. With your own DATUM gateway and Bitcoin Knots node, you build the block template and the pool only coordinates the reward. That keeps block-building spread across many hands. Here it costs 0% and earns a bonus.
          
          The DATUM subsidy →
        
          
        
          
          4
          
          How TIDES pays
          
          
          TIDES keeps a rolling window of accepted shares worth about eight network-difficulties of work. When the pool finds a block, every address holding work in the window is paid its proportion. There are no rounds to hop between and nothing resets when a block lands.
          
          TIDES explained →
        
          
        
          
          5
          
          Paid in the block itself
          
          
          The pool never holds your coins. Each payout is an output of the block’s own coinbase, sent to your address. There is no balance, no threshold and no withdrawal, and anyone can check every payout on chain.
          
          Non-custodial payouts →
        
          
        
          
          6
          
          A pool that caps itself
          
          
          The public stratum is held to 15% of network hashrate. Past that, new stratum connections are relayed to other pools and paid there. Miners on their own DATUM gateway are never counted, because building your own templates is not what centralizes a chain.
          
          The 15% self-cap →
        
          
      
          
      
          
        
          
          
          From zero to mining, the DATUM way
          
          
            
            
          1. Get a payout address. Any wallet that gives you a bc1… address on this chain. That address is your username; there is nothing to register.
            2. 
            
          2. Run your own node. Bitcoin Knots for this chain, with server=1 and a cookie or RPC user your gateway can read. Your node is what chooses the transactions in your blocks.
            3. 
            
          3. Start a DATUM gateway. Build one (iohzrd is the recommended build), paste in the Lazarus host, port and pubkey from the Connect page, and set mining.pool_address to your address.
            4. 
            
          4. Point your miner at your gateway. Any Siacoin BLAKE2b ASIC, aimed at your own gateway’s stratum port, with the username address.worker.
            5. 
            
          5. Watch it work. Your gateway shows up under Gateways and your address under Miners. You pay 0%, you build your own blocks, and every block the pool finds pays you the DATUM bonus on top.

          Words you will meet
          
          
          
          
          Hashrate
          Hashes tried per second. 1 TH/s is a trillion a second; 1 PH/s is a thousand TH/s.
          
          
          Share
          A hash good enough to prove you are working, though not good enough to be a block. Shares are how the pool measures your work.
          
          
          Difficulty
          How hard the network’s target is. It retargets so blocks keep their pace as hashrate comes and goes.
          
          
          Coinbase
          The first transaction in a block, which creates the reward. Here it pays miners directly.
          
          
          Block template
          The draft block a miner hashes: which transactions are in it, and who the coinbase pays.
          
          
          Window
          The stretch of recent shares TIDES pays on. Here it is eight network-difficulties of work.
          
          
          Gateway
          Your own DATUM software. It talks to your node for templates and to the pool for the reward split.
          
          
          Orphan
          A valid block that lost the race to another found at the same moment. It pays nothing.
          
          
          
        
          
      
          
    
          
  
          

  
          
    
          
      
          
        
          How it works
          
        
          How your share becomes a payout.
          
        
          For newcomers and operators alike. Your ASIC never sees any of this — it hashes a plain BLAKE2b header.
          
      
          
      
            
        
          1. 
          01
          
            You hash, the pool counts
            
          
            Point a miner at the public stratum, or at a DATUM gateway you run. Either way your username is your Bitcoin address. Every share you send is rebuilt into a full BLAKE2b header and hashed by Prime; accepted work is credited to your address in the TIDES window.
            
        
            2. 
        
          2. 
          02
          
            The window decides the split
            
          
            The window holds the last 8 × network difficulty of accepted work — days of pool work at typical hashrate, not the last hour. A found block pays each address in proportion to its share of that window, less its fee. A new rig starts near 0% and ramps as its work enters and older work ages out.
            
        
            3. 
        
          3. 
          03
          
            The block pays you directly
            
          
            Prime hands every gateway the same coinbase output list — the one shown under Next payout. A share whose coinbase pays anything else is refused, so whoever finds the block, it pays the window. There is no pool balance to withdraw and no operator to trust with your coins.
            
        
            4. 
        
          4. 
          04
          
            DATUM work is paid the bonus
            
          
            
        
            5. 
      
          
      
          
    
          
  
          

  
          
    
          
      
          
        
          Decentralize the hashrate
          
        
          Other pools that speak DATUM and TIDES.
          
        
          No single pool should hold a third of this chain. We cap our own stratum at 15% of the network; hashrate behind a miner’s own DATUM gateway builds its own blocks and is not counted. Past that line, new stratum connections are relayed to one of these pools and paid there. Each one lets you build your own block through a DATUM gateway and pays by a TIDES window, so splitting your fleet across them costs you nothing in principle. Keep one as a failover in your miner either way.
