Wallet

Lazarus Key

A hardware wallet app for Bitcoin BLAKE2b. It is Block's Bitkey app, opened up and pointed at XBT: the same Bitkey device in your hand, the same keys, signing on the chain this network actually runs.

Why

Why a hardware wallet for XBT

XBT shares Bitcoin's history, which is what makes it valuable and what makes it dangerous. The key that controls an address on Bitcoin controls the same address here, so a wallet that does not understand both chains can cost you coins on the one you were not thinking about.

Lazarus Key takes the wallet Block built — two-of-three signing, a hardware device that has to approve every spend, a paper kit that gets you back in if the phone dies — and makes all of it work on BLAKE2b. Your spending key never leaves the Bitkey. Nothing is signed without a tap.

Features

What it does

Hardware signing

Every send is signed by your Bitkey over NFC. The app holds one key, the device holds another, and neither can move your money alone.

Replay protection

Sends are signed with a unified sighash that SHA-256 Bitcoin rejects. A transaction made here cannot be replayed onto the other chain against your pre-fork coins.

Your node, not ours

On first launch it asks for your own Electrum server. The Lazarus Pool server is offered only if you say you have none, and you can change it whenever you like.

Nothing reported

No crash reporting, no usage analytics, no firmware telemetry. Your wallet addresses are never registered with anyone. What the app knows stays on the phone.

Long maturity, handled

Mined coins need 6,480 confirmations until block 979,920. The balance counts them separately so you always know what is spendable now.

Emergency Exit Kit

The same paper kit as upstream. It restores your wallet on a new phone with no server, no account and nobody's permission.

XBT prices

Rates and the price chart come from a market that actually trades XBT, not from a Bitcoin price feed.

Biometric lock

Fingerprint or face unlock on the app, plus a fingerprint on the Bitkey itself before it will sign.

Recovery, if you want it

Block's cloud backup, recovery contacts and inheritance can be switched on. Off by default, and off means off.

Your choice

Two ways to run it

A Bitkey wallet is protected by three keys: one on your phone, one on the device, and one held by Block. That third key is why a brand-new wallet has to be created through their servers once. What happens after that is up to you.

Setup

Getting started

You will need

An Android phone with NFC, and a Bitkey hardware device. Bitkey is sold by Block at bitkey.world; Lazarus does not sell hardware and earns nothing from it. Ideally, also your own Bitcoin Knots node with an XBT-aware Electrum server.

If you already have a Bitkey wallet

  1. In the official Bitkey app, find your Emergency Exit Kit — the PDF saved to your cloud drive when you set the wallet up.
  2. Install Lazarus Key and choose I already have a wallet, then Import using Emergency Exit Kit.
  3. Enter the backup code from the kit and tap your Bitkey when asked. Your balance appears once the wallet syncs.

Move your XBT first. A coin that existed before block 961,640 sits at the same address on both chains. Spending it in the official Bitkey app produces a signature that can be replayed here. Send your XBT with Lazarus Key first — that splits the coins — and then use either app freely.

If your Bitkey is new

  1. Install Lazarus Key and choose Set up a new wallet. It will explain that creating the third key needs Block's servers once.
  2. Tap your Bitkey to create its keys and finish setup.
  3. Save your Emergency Exit Kit somewhere safe. Print it.
  4. If you want the app to stop talking to Block, turn off Block account in Settings.

Decentralisation

Run your own node

A wallet has to ask someone what the chain says. Whoever answers sees every address you ask about and decides what your wallet believes. On a young network, a handful of servers answering for everybody is how a chain quietly ends up under somebody's thumb.

Lazarus Key asks for your server on first launch for that reason. You need Bitcoin Knots 29.4.2 or later with an Electrum server that understands 164-byte headers — electrs or Fulcrum, built for this chain — and then point the app at ssl://your-host:50002. The chain page has the node setup.

If you do not have one yet, the app will use electrum.lazarus-xbt.xyz and tell you plainly what that means. It is a starting point, not a destination.

Installing

  1. Download the APK to your phone and compare its checksum with the value above.
  2. Open it. Android will ask whether to allow installs from your browser or file manager — this is what sideloading means, and it is worth understanding before you agree.
  3. Open Lazarus Key and follow the setup steps.

Every build is signed with the same key, so later versions install over earlier ones and keep your wallet. A build signed with a different key will refuse to install over this one — which is the protection working, not a fault.

FAQ

Questions

Does this work with the Bitkey I already own?

Yes. It is the same device, the same keys and the same Emergency Exit Kit. Nothing on the hardware is changed or reflashed, and the official Bitkey app keeps working for your bitcoin.

Can I use both apps at once?

Yes, and many people will: the official app for bitcoin, Lazarus Key for XBT. They install side by side. Move your XBT with Lazarus Key first so the coins on the two chains are no longer the same coins.

Is Block involved in this?

No. Bitkey's source is published under the MIT licence, which is what allows a fork, but Block did not build, review or endorse this and cannot support it. Do not contact them about it.

Does the app send anything to Block?

By default, one thing: a check for Bitkey firmware updates, which sends your device's serial and firmware version, because that is where firmware is published. Crash reporting, usage analytics and firmware telemetry have all been removed. Your wallet addresses are never sent anywhere.

What happens if Lazarus disappears?

Your money is unaffected. It lives on the chain, the spending keys are on your phone and your Bitkey, and the Emergency Exit Kit restores the wallet without any server of ours. Point the app at your own node and nothing of ours is in the path at all.

Why can I not buy or sell in the app?

Those buttons go to Block's partners, who deal in SHA-256 bitcoin. A purchase would arrive on the other chain, at an address this wallet can see but not spend from. They are switched off on purpose.

Why can I not spend without my Bitkey?

Spending limits without hardware rely on Block's servers co-signing, and their signature is for the wrong chain — XBT nodes reject it. Every send here needs a tap.

Some of my balance says "maturing".

Mined coins cannot be spent until they mature. Until block 979,920 that is 6,480 confirmations rather than 100, so freshly mined XBT shows separately until it is ready.

Is the source available?

Yes, all of it: github.com/AwokenLazarus/lazarus-key, under the MIT licence. The upstream app it is forked from is proto-at-block/bitkey, published by Block under the same licence. XBT.md in the repository is the record of what was changed and why, and NOTICE.md covers the licence, the trademarks and the absence of any warranty.

A wallet you cannot inspect is a wallet you are taking on faith. Every line of this one is published at github.com/AwokenLazarus/lazarus-key, including the changes that make it work on XBT and the ones that stop it reporting anything to anybody.
