Guide

Start mining XBT

Mining here means running your own node and DATUM gateway: you build the block, the pool only splits the coinbase, and there is no fee. There is no account to make and nothing to register — your payout address is your identity on the pool.

Before you connect

  1. Make a fresh wallet

    Never mine to an address that has ever held real bitcoin on the SHA-256 chain. History up to block 961,640 is shared between the two chains, so reusing an address links your XBT activity to your BTC and can expose you to replay confusion. A brand new wallet, used only for XBT, avoids all of it.

  2. Pick your hardware

    Anything that hashes BLAKE2b: purpose-built ASICs, GPUs, or a CPU to test the plumbing. SHA-256 bitcoin miners cannot mine this chain at all.

  3. Plan for your own node

    A DATUM gateway needs a node beside it, and a node needs to sync. Start that first: it is the only part of this that takes hours rather than minutes. Accepted work follows your address, so you can move a rig over the moment the gateway is up.

Recommended

Your own DATUM gateway

Your node decides what goes in the block. Lazarus only issues the coinbase split and verifies your shares, takes no fee from you, and credits you part of what public-stratum miners pay.

  1. Run a node

    Bitcoin Knots 29.4.2 or later, built for BLAKE2b, fully synced. It builds every template you mine, so its rules are the ones that decide whether your blocks are valid. See node setup.

  2. Install a DATUM gateway

    Use a gateway build that speaks this chain — the CONVOY, iohzrd or FlyTheElephant fork, or the Rust lazarus-gateway in our repository.

  3. Point it at Lazarus Prime

    In the gateway's config, set the pool host and port below and your payout address. Your miners then connect to your gateway, not to us.

    datum.lazarus-xbt.xyz:28915

  4. Check it landed

    Your gateway appears on the pool's gateway list within a few minutes of its first share, and as a planet on the galaxy map once it helps find a block.

What you get

No pool fee. Your whole window share is paid to your address in the coinbase.

Your template. You choose the transactions, the fee policy and the ordering. Nobody can make you mine a block you did not build.

The whole window is yours. While the hosted stratum winds down, part of what it still pays is credited to DATUM miners in proportion to their work; after it closes, the window belongs entirely to gateway operators.

The pool's public gateway is itself a DATUM gateway on this Prime, so you can see exactly what your setup should look like.

Winding down

Leaving the public stratum

The hosted stratum was a way to start in a minute while your node synced. It is being retired: work sent through it will not continue to be supported or paid, so move while it still runs.

  1. Sync a node

    Bitcoin Knots 29.4.2 or later, built for BLAKE2b. Keep mining on the stratum meanwhile — this is the long part. See node setup.

  2. Bring up a gateway beside it

    Install a DATUM gateway, point it at your node and at datum.lazarus-xbt.xyz:28915, and set your payout address. Full steps.

  3. Move your miners across

    Change each miner's pool URL from the hosted stratum to your gateway's own stratum address. Do one rig first and watch it accept shares.

  4. Check the window followed you

    Your address page on the pool should show the same accepted work, now arriving through your gateway. Nothing resets: the window is keyed to your address, not your connection.

Why it is ending

On a pool stratum the pool builds every block and the miner only supplies hashes. That is the arrangement this chain forked to get away from.

Nodes are also deciding to invalidate coinbase payouts to addresses that hash through a pool's stratum instead of their own node. That would apply on every pool, so moving to a different stratum would not avoid it.

Running your own gateway costs no fee, and settles the question for good.

Long odds

Solo mining

Find a block and the whole thing is yours: subsidy and every transaction fee in it, paid to your address in that block's coinbase — built by your node, through your own gateway.

Run the same gateway in solo mode: your node builds the block, and the coinbase pays you alone. There is no shared window and no smoothing, so nothing arrives until you find a block. With a small rig that can be a very long wait, and that is the point — it is the one path where the reward is never split.

Solo is a setting on your own gateway, not a different pool to point at. The gateway source documents the flag.

Is solo sensible for me?

Work out how often your hashrate would find a block at the current network total (the pool page shows both figures). If the answer is measured in years, pooled mining pays the same on average but in usable pieces.

Infrastructure

Running a node

Required for a DATUM gateway, useful for everyone else: your node is what decides which chain you are on.

Which build

Bitcoin Knots 29.4.2 or later, built for BLAKE2b headers. Our node directory has the install script and a reference bitcoin.conf.

Why the version matters now

29.4.2 enforces the long coinbase maturity rule from block 973,440. A node on older rules will accept blocks the network rejects and keep mining on top of them, so everything it produces there is wasted work. If your gateway's blocks are being refused, your node is the first thing to check.

Keep it reachable

Templates are only as fresh as your node's view of the tip. Give it peers, let it accept inbound connections if you can, and watch that its height matches the explorer.

Just want a wallet?

You do not need a node to hold XBT. Point an Electrum wallet that understands this chain's headers at the public server:

electrum.lazarus-xbt.xyz:50002:s

SSL, Electrum protocol 1.8. Use a wallet created fresh for XBT.

Check your mining

Your address page on the pool shows live hashrate, window share, every block that has paid you and what is still maturing.

If something is wrong

Common first problems

My miner connects but nothing is accepted

Check the username is a valid XBT address, not a worker name or an email. Shares from an unusable username are refused. If you use address.worker, the part before the dot must be the address.

My hashrate on the pool looks lower than on my miner

Pool-side hashrate is inferred from accepted shares over a window, so it lags and wobbles. Judge it over an hour, not a minute. A persistent gap of more than a few percent usually means rejects: look at the reject reason on your miner.

My gateway's blocks are being rejected

Almost always an un-upgraded node. Run Knots 29.4.2 or later, then restart the gateway so it takes fresh templates. Until then its work cannot become a valid block, and it will not be credited.

I mined to the wrong address

Payouts go into the coinbase of blocks as they are found, so anything already paid is final and on chain. Change the username on your miner and future work pays the new address.
