Whitepaper · XBT StreamStream: pay as it arrives

A way for people and AI agents to pay for a service piece by piece, while it is being delivered: an answer token by token, a file chunk by chunk, an API call by call. Each piece costs one signature. Stop at any moment and nothing more is owed.

Draft 1 · 7 October 2026 · Lazarus. Working on test networks; not yet on XBT mainnet.

Lightning made payments fast. Stream makes them continuous.

1The problem

More and more of what we buy arrives over time: the tokens of a model's answer, the minutes of a stream, the pages of a dataset, the thousandth call to an API. Machines are now the buyers as often as people, and the prices are tiny: fractions of a cent, thousands of times an hour.

Every existing way to pay for that asks someone to trust someone. Pay up front and you trust the seller to deliver. Pay afterwards and the seller trusts you. A subscription or an API key trusts a company with your card. An on-chain payment for each piece is honest but far too slow and too costly.

Stream removes the trust from the middle. Buyer and seller settle continuously, in amounts as small as the service itself, and either can walk away at any moment having lost at most one piece.

2The idea

Lock coins once. For every piece delivered, the buyer signs a slightly larger running total. Settle once, at the end, for the last total signed.

That is the whole protocol. Everything else in this paper is how to make it safe, fast, and usable by software that should never be trusted with a key.

3The channel

A Stream channel is one ordinary XBT output with two ways to spend it.

Money only moves one way, from buyer to seller, and each new state pays the seller more than the last. That single fact removes most of what makes payment channels hard:

Nothing to revoke
An old state pays the seller less, so it has no reason to publish one, and the buyer cannot publish anything without the seller's signature. There are no penalty transactions and no watchtowers.
The buyer can go offline
After it signs, the buyer has nothing to watch. If the seller disappears, the refund path returns everything once the expiry height passes.
The seller has one duty
Close before expiry. Software does this with a safety margin of a day (144 blocks) and the newest state in hand.
One round trip to open
The seller publishes one key. Each channel uses a fresh key derived from it, the buyer's key and the expiry, so no two channels look alike on chain and the seller does not need to be asked first.
Bound to XBT
XBT shares its history with Bitcoin, so every Stream signature commits to XBT's own signature-hash flag (SIGHASH_ALL|UNIFIED, 0x21). A state signed here cannot be replayed on the SHA-256 chain.

4Paying over HTTP

The web has had a status code for this since 1997: 402 Payment Required. The x402 standard gives it a shape: the server answers with a price and terms, and the client retries with a payment in a header. Stream is a payment scheme for x402, so a client that speaks x402 can pay with it.

Each receipt is signed by the seller and bound to the hash of the exact request it paid for, so a buyer can prove what it bought and a seller cannot charge one request twice.

5Paying only for what checks out

Paying per piece only helps if a bad piece can be caught. For anything that can be cut into chunks, a file, a dataset, a long result, the seller first signs a manifest: the Merkle root of every chunk it promises to send. Then the stream begins.

6A wallet for agents

An AI model should be able to spend, but never to hold a key: a model can be tricked by what it reads. So the agent never signs. It asks a wallet to pay, and the wallet decides.

The wallet speaks MCP, the protocol AI agents already use for tools, so any agent that can call a tool can pay. Each seller gets its own channel sized to that seller's budget. Payments above the owner's threshold wait for the owner's signature from another device. The policy itself is signed by the owner, so the agent cannot rewrite it.

7Hubs

A direct channel is ideal for a seller you use often. To reach many sellers with one channel, a buyer can pay through a hub.

The lock is an adaptor signature: the buyer's state for the hub is incomplete until the seller signs for the matching payment, and that signature is what completes it. There is no new script and no hash puzzle, so even amounts too small for an on-chain output are still enforceable, because what settles on chain is the channel's total, not each payment.

8Beside Lightning

Lightning is the right tool for paying anyone, once. Stream is the right tool for paying a service continuously. They are not rivals; the same wallet pays both, under the same budgets.

LightningStream

A hub that also runs Lightning can bridge the two, so a Stream buyer can reach a Lightning seller. That is planned, not built.

9Numbers

Measured on test networks with our reference and Rust implementations.

Per paid call
About 4 ms in Rust. The pure-Python reference: 10.1 ms median, 12.1 ms at the 99th percentile.
1,000 calls
At 150 sat each: 150,000 sat paid with about 1,200 sat of on-chain fees for the channel's whole life, an overhead of 0.8%.
Refund
With the seller gone, the buyer recovered 199,400 of 200,000 sat after expiry; the rest was the transaction fee.
Paid inference
A model paid for token by token through a hub ran at 0.89 to 0.96 of its unpaid speed.

10Status and limits

Works
Open, pay, stream, close, refund and hub routing, on XBT's regtest and a private test network. A Claude agent given only the wallet's tools bought an analytics API, refused a tampered chunk, and the seller received exactly the 8,500 sat it charged.
Reviewed
An internal security review found three critical and four high-severity issues. All are fixed and covered by tests. No independent audit yet.
Not yet
XBT mainnet, deliberately on hold; a published specification; the Lightning bridge. Apps for Umbrel and StartOS are being tested on the real platforms.
Trade-offs
Funds are locked per channel until it closes. Money flows one way per channel. The seller must be online once before expiry to close. Opening and closing are public transactions.

On the wire, Stream is XBT's binding of the x402 v2 batch-settlement scheme.

In one sentence. Lock once, sign a bigger number for every piece you receive, settle once: a buyer can pay for anything that arrives over time, a seller is paid for exactly what it delivered, and neither has to trust the other for more than one piece.
